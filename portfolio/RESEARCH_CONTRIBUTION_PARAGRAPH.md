## 180-220-word Personal Statement version

I wanted to study a reliability problem that accuracy alone cannot answer: when a scientific claim verifier is wrong, can it identify which predictions deserve human review? I set that research objective and the project's integrity requirements, including leakage controls, frozen-result preservation, critical review, and honest reporting of unfavorable outcomes. The technical work was substantially AI-assisted; AI systems contributed to method discussions, implementation, debugging, experiment execution, statistical checks, reproducibility audits, and writing.

The project compared a matched NLI-only error ranker with Evidence-Aware Risk Scoring, which adds retrieval-margin and evidence-conflict signals. The historical result was deliberately reported as mixed. EARS improved AURC from 0.300833 to 0.264987, yet worsened selective risk at 80% coverage from 0.428571 to 0.457143. Because the frozen criterion required both endpoints to improve, H1 remained inconclusive. On SciFact Dev, the metric directions reversed and both paired confidence intervals crossed zero. I therefore retained the negative conclusion: the evidence does not establish stable incremental benefit or cross-dataset generalization.

My role is best described as human research direction and accountability, not unaided technical production. I requested correction of provenance, label-isolation, statistical-language, licensing, and release-quality problems and remain responsible for the accuracy of public claims. The experience taught me that credible research includes preserving inconvenient results, tracing failure mechanisms, and stating precisely what the evidence cannot support.
